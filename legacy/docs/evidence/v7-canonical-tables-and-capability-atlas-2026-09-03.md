## 1. Two corrections made before the atlas was trusted

### 1.1 The canonical `enum_failure` flag is a disguised parsed filter

The canonical history table carries one `enum_failure` boolean that fires when
**role, position, lane, or leaver status** is missing or outside the frozen
vocabulary. In this corpus `position`, `role` and `lane` are populated
essentially only for parsed matches:

| `enum_failure` | `is_parsed` | position missing | rows (120-player probe) |
|---|---|---|---:|
| false | true | no | 61,235 |
| true | false | yes | 14,974 |
| true | true | yes | 2,036 |

There is no row with `enum_failure = false` and `is_parsed = false`. Using that
flag as the structural-eligibility gate therefore silently conditions the
entire research population on `isParsed = true` under a field name that does
not say so — exactly the failure mode that learning 13 forbids. The first
version of this atlas did precisely that and reported 429,800 eligible rows.

The corrected structural gate uses only **match context** — game mode, lobby
type, leaver status — which is present for every row regardless of parsed
availability. Role-context availability is now reported as a separate,
explicitly parsed-dependent property.

| gate | eligible rows |
|---|---:|
| structurally observable (mode/lobby/leaver known) | 580,323 |
| product context (ordinary lobby, finished by the player) | 562,250 |
| ... of which role/position/lane are also known | 429,549 |

### 1.2 Turbo is the majority of the corpus, not an edge case

| stratum | rows | median duration | p95 duration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `TURBO` | 378,483 | 1,485 s | 2,298 s |
| `STANDARD` (all-pick ranked, single draft, captains, random draft) | 201,840 | 2,417 s | 3,605 s |

Turbo runs a different gold and experience economy on a different clock. It is
retained as an explicit context stratum and must never be silently pooled with
standard modes — but the reach consequence of *excluding* it is severe, and is
quantified in section 4.

## 2. Verified provider semantics

Established from payload evidence over the corpus, not from field names.

| field | verified semantics | confidence |
|---|---|---|
| `radiant_networth_leads[i]` | cumulative signed Radiant-minus-Dire net-worth **level** at minute `i`; not an increment | HIGH |
| `radiant_kills[i]` / `dire_kills[i]` | kills scored by that team **during** minute `i` (interval) | HIGH |
| trajectory length | `ceil(duration/60) + 1` for 122,449 of 124,535 rows; one greater for 2,086; the three arrays always share a length | HIGH |
| index 0 | covers the pre-horn period and the first minute, so it legitimately carries kills; 12% of rows have a non-zero value there | HIGH |
| `kill_events[].time` | seconds from horn; negative values occur and are genuine pre-horn kills | HIGH |
| `stats.kill_events` count | equals the history `kills` count for 118,303 of 124,535 rows (95.0%); the residual skews low by one | MEDIUM |
| `stats.assist_events` count | equals the history `assists` count for only 64,914 of 124,535 rows (52.1%), differing in **both** directions | LOW |
| `position` / `role` / `lane` | three independent provider concepts, preserved separately; never mapped through the legacy OpenDota role vocabulary | HIGH |

Consequences, applied as fail-closed rules in `services/api/app/player_analysis_v7/research/tables.py`:

- The net-worth lead is reported from Radiant's point of view. A Dire player's
  own lead is its negation. `player_networth_lead()` performs that flip;
  omitting it would invert the sign of every comeback and lead-retention
  estimand. Orientation resolved for 124,535 of 124,535 trajectory rows.
- Trajectories are clipped to `min(len(array), ceil(duration/60) + 1)` rather
  than trusting either the array length or the duration alone.
- `assist_events` may be used for **timing and participation shape**. It must
  not be used as an assist count that is expected to reconcile with the history
  table. Any candidate needing an exact assist count uses one source only, and
  states which.

## 3. Fields unavailable by construction

`GetParsedAcquisitionBatch` v1.0.0 did not request them, so the completed
corpus does not contain them and no candidate may be designed around one:

- the player's own `networthPerMinute`, `lastHitsPerMinute`, `deniesPerMinute`,
  and `heroDamagePerMinute` trajectories;
- the player's own `deathEvents` timings;
- anything playback-derived (prohibited surface);
- rank, MMR, bracket, leaderboard, IMP, behaviour, smurf, award, and
  proprietary prediction fields (prohibited surface).

Personal farm-curve, personal-damage-curve, and death-timing families are
therefore **not reachable** without a new collection. Team net-worth-lead and
team-kill trajectories, the player's own kill/assist/item timings, and the full
365-day history table are reachable.

## 4. Reach denominators

```text
source frame accounts                          4,135
frozen cohort                                  1,200
research splits (DISCOVERY + CANDIDATE_TEST)     900   (600 / 300)
accounts returning at least one history row      838
accounts with at least one product-context row   835
parsed-detail accounts collected                 235   (116 / 119)
```

62 accounts returned no history row at all; 60 of those are anonymous
profiles, which are excluded by design. Three further accounts have rows but
none in product context. The honest **product-eligible** denominator for reach
claims is therefore **835 of 900 sampled accounts (92.8%)**, and every reach
figure below uses the full 900 unless stated otherwise.

The cohort was drawn from the V6.1 session-drift fixed frame, which is a frame
of *active public* accounts. This population is more active than Dota players
at large: median 526 product-context matches per account over 365 days. Reach
percentages here describe sufficiently active eligible users, which is exactly
the population the 80–90% target is stated over — but they are not population
estimates for all Dota players, and must never be presented as such.

### 5.1 The single most consequential structural fact

A candidate restricted to standard modes reaches at most **59%** of sampled
DISCOVERY accounts at a 30-match support bar, and 45% at 100. A candidate that
pools Turbo and standard with mode as a context covariate reaches **90%** and
**84%**.

No portfolio can plausibly deliver "≥3 Findings for 80–90% of sufficiently
active eligible users" out of standard-mode-only candidates. Either candidates
model mode as context and pool, or the product target is unreachable — and the
correct response to that would be to redesign candidates, never to relax a
statistical standard. This constraint is handed to candidate design as a hard
requirement rather than discovered late.

Turbo is not a free lunch: it compresses the clock, so any minute-indexed
estimand must be expressed on a mode-appropriate scale (or on a normalised
game-progress scale) rather than at fixed wall-clock minutes.

## 6. Parsed-selection structure

Parsed availability is close to deterministic given observable context: in
every observed context stratum where `position` is known, the parsed share is
1.00; where it is missing, the share collapses to 0.07–0.35. Parsed
availability and role-context availability are the same mechanism, not two.

Population-wide, parsed matches are 79% of history rows and the per-account
parsed share has median 0.80 and p25 0.70 — parsed evidence is broadly
available rather than a narrow slice. The corpus limitation is different and
must not be confused with it: **parsed detail was only collected for the 256
predeclared subset accounts**, of which 235 produced data. Within that subset,
92% of accounts have ≥100 parsed product matches.

So a parsed-dependent candidate has:

- good **product** reach (median account has ~80% of its matches parsed);
- limited **research** reach in this corpus (235 accounts, 116 in DISCOVERY),
  which constrains how precisely between-player heterogeneity can be estimated
  for parsed-only candidates, not whether the Finding could ship.

Both denominators must be reported separately whenever a parsed-dependent
candidate's reach is quoted.

## 7. Context and confounding structure

- **Patch**: three game versions — 180 (149,218 rows), 181 (12,046), 182
  (419,059). Patch 182 dominates but 180 is a substantial secondary stratum, so
  patch is available as a covariate and is not degenerate.
- **Position**: near-uniform across the five positions (81,866–97,113 rows
  each). The median account has played **all five**, so position is not a
  stable per-player trait: position mix is itself behaviour, and a candidate
  may not treat "the player's position" as a fixed attribute.
- **Role**: `CORE` 284,913, `HARD_SUPPORT` 90,505, `LIGHT_SUPPORT` 85,344.
- **Lane**: `SAFE_LANE` 191,366, `OFF_LANE` 167,616, `MID_LANE` 98,720,
  `JUNGLE` 1,758, `ROAMING` 945, `UNKNOWN` 357. Jungle and roaming are too
  sparse to carry a candidate on their own.
- **Lobby**: `UNRANKED` 408,099, `RANKED` 171,062, plus 1,162 practice and
  battle-cup rows excluded from product context.
- **Leaver**: 563,369 clean; 16,954 rows with a non-clean status are excluded
  from product context, since a match the player did not finish cannot support
  a claim about how they responded during it.
- **Cross-player match overlap**: 8,182 of 571,929 history matches (1.4%) and
  923 of 127,692 parsed matches appear for two sampled accounts. Small, but it
  breaks independence for any estimand that pools across accounts at the match
  level.

Finding discovery may begin, under three standing constraints carried from this
atlas:

1. pool Turbo with standard modes using mode as context, or accept that the
   portfolio target is unreachable;
2. never gate a research population on `enum_failure`, role, position, or lane
   without declaring the parsed-selection mechanism that comes with it;
3. design no candidate that needs a personal farm, damage, or death-timing
   trajectory.
