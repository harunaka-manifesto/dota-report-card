## Redundancy is not the problem

Two measures, both on DISCOVERY. `Jaccard` is the share of players qualified by
either candidate that are qualified by both — the portfolio-relevant question,
since a high value means two Findings land on the same people. `rho` is the
Spearman correlation of the per-player effects themselves.

| pair | Jaccard | rho | reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| post_loss_requeue_latency ↔ post_loss_session_continuation | 0.342 | −0.580 | the only genuinely redundant pair; stopping and taking a long gap are close to the same act |
| lane_recovery_participation ↔ lead_retention | 0.286 | — | both are near-null, so this is overlap of noise |
| fight_timing_centroid ↔ purchase_tempo | 0.275 | +0.466 | two facets of "when does your game happen" |
| position_flexibility ↔ purchase_tempo | 0.263 | — | shared parsed subset, not shared meaning |
| duration_tempo ↔ post_loss_requeue_latency | 0.258 | — | both partly tempo |
| hero_novelty ↔ post_loss_requeue_latency | 0.253 | — | weak |

**No pair exceeds a Jaccard of 0.35.** The candidates are close to independent
in who they qualify. That is normally good news, and here it is the opposite:
it means coverage cannot be improved by picking less-redundant candidates,
because they are already barely redundant. Near-independence is exactly why the
portfolio behaves like a binomial over low per-candidate rates.

**The best five-Finding portfolio this corpus supports delivers ≥3 qualified
Findings to 15.4% of information-eligible accounts at the provisional 0.01
level, and 27.7% at 0.05.** The target is 80–90%.

Three structural facts follow directly:

1. **Every strong portfolio is history-only.** The top three carry no
   parsed-dependent candidate at all. Parsed candidates have excellent reach
   *within* the parsed subset but the subset is 116 of 600 DISCOVERY accounts,
   so they contribute almost nothing to portfolio-level coverage in this corpus.
2. **Every strong portfolio leans on the post-loss family.** All five of the top
   portfolios contain at least two post-loss candidates, and the best contains
   three — including the one genuinely redundant pair. The portfolio is not
   diverse because the diverse alternatives are weaker, not because diversity
   was not sought.
3. **The uncovered population is not a segment, it is the middle.** 35.7% of
   information-eligible accounts qualify for nothing across all twelve
   candidates. They are not concentrated in a role, a mode, a patch, or an
   activity band: they are the players whose behaviour is close to the
   population average, which is what a significance gate against the population
   average is designed to exclude. See
   `docs/evidence/v7-qualification-ceiling-2026-09-04.md` — the share is a
   closed-form function of `tau/SE`, and no candidate selection changes it.

## Portfolio options for the owner

The four archetypal options the research plan asks for do not all exist here.
What the evidence actually supports:

| option | five candidates | P(≥3) 0.01 | P(≥3) 0.05 | P(all 5) 0.01 | diversity | parsed | confidence | main tradeoff |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **A — highest reach** | session_continuation, hero_switch, requeue_latency, hero_novelty, duration_tempo | **0.154** | 0.277 | 0.006 | low — three post-loss, one redundant pair | none | A A B C C | buys reach with two candidates whose nulls the tournament could not certify |
| **B — highest confidence** | session_continuation, hero_switch, requeue_latency, transfer_risk, purchase_tempo | 0.072 | 0.152 | 0.000 | moderate | one | A A B B B — the only all-A/B option | less than half the reach of option A, and still contains the redundant pair |
| **C — most diverse** | session_continuation, hero_switch, transfer_risk, hero_novelty, purchase_tempo | 0.066 | 0.135 | 0.000 | highest available — setback, hero choice, novelty, build tempo | one | A A B B C | the lowest coverage of the four; breaking the redundant pair costs more than the redundancy did |
| **D — balanced** | session_continuation, hero_switch, requeue_latency, transfer_risk, hero_novelty | 0.131 | 0.230 | 0.002 | moderate | none | A A B B C | still three post-loss candidates; hero_novelty's null is anticonservative even under an i.i.d. permutation |

Note what the ordering says: the highest-confidence option has **less than half**
the coverage of the highest-reach option, and the most diverse option is the
worst of the four. Diversity and statistical confidence both cost reach here,
because the candidates that supply them are the weaker ones.

There is no option that reaches the target. Presenting one would require either
a different qualification concept or a relaxed standard, and the second is
forbidden.

## What would actually move coverage

In descending order of expected effect, and none of it enacted here:

1. **Change the qualification concept.** Reporting a per-player estimate with an
   honest interval, rather than gating on distinguishability from the population
   average, converts reach from ~15% to roughly the structural reach — 92–95%
   for the post-loss family — with the honesty carried by the interval instead
   of by a filter. This is a product decision with real costs: a Finding that
   says "your effect is +2 points, and we cannot tell it from zero" is a
   different product promise. It is the owner's call, not research's.
2. **Give level estimands a defensible uncertainty model.** `duration_tempo`
   (`tau/SE` 9.69) and `purchase_tempo` (7.46) already carry more than enough
   signal; they are graded down only because their standard errors cannot
   absorb a drifting 365-day window. Shortening the window, modelling drift as a
   context term, or recasting them as within-player contrasts are all live.
3. **Collect parsed detail for the whole cohort.** It would raise the parsed
   candidates from 116 to ~840 usable accounts and make parsed candidates
   portfolio-relevant. It requires a new collection and therefore owner
   authorisation, and it does not fix the ceiling in point 1.
4. **Find more distinct behavioural setback responses.** The cheapest genuine
   candidate work, but the tournament's own arithmetic says even a perfect new
   candidate at the best measured `tau/SE` adds only a few points of P(≥3).
